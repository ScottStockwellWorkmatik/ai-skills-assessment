How to self-assess. Rate yourself on each domain. 0 means no awareness; 4 means organisational champion. Be honest: the result drives your development plan. A manager or peer can complete the same form independently, then both files are compared in the Team rollup tab.

Keep the downloaded file: it is your record and the input to the Team rollup tab. For the person being assessed, use initials or a staff code if your policy requires pseudonymised data.

Combine results. Drop in several downloaded XLSX files. Files are read in your browser and are not uploaded. Self-assessments build the team heatmap; manager and peer files for the same name are compared to flag gaps of more than one level.

    The five proficiency levels

    Assessment process

    1. Self-assessment. Each person completes the Self-assessment tab and downloads the XLSX. About 10 minutes.
    2. Second rater. A manager or peer completes the same form independently, choosing Manager or Peer feedback and entering the same name.
    3. Calibration. Load both files in Team rollup. Differences of more than one level are flagged for a development conversation.
    4. Team rollup. Load everyone's files to see averages and gaps by domain, and prioritise cohort learning.
    5. Records. Keep the XLSX files and resulting development plans as part of your internal records of AI literacy measures. The European Commission says internal records are sufficient and no certificate is required. Check with your legal adviser what your organisation must retain.

    About your data

    This page has no server, no account and no tracking. Ratings exist only in this browser tab and are lost when you close or refresh it, unless you download the XLSX. Files loaded into Team rollup are read locally and never uploaded.
