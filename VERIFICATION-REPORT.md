# EU AI Act verification report

Checked 24 October 2026 for framework version 1.1.0. Each finding gives its source (title, owner, date, direct URL) directly beneath it. Not legal advice: have the final wording reviewed by the client's legal adviser.

**Headline:** the Act was amended in July 2026 by the Digital Omnibus on AI, and that changes three things in the June version: the Article 4 duty, the high-risk application dates, and several indicators that placed provider duties on deployers. These are corrected in `data/framework.json`.

## What changed in the law since the June version

**Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI)** amends Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. EUR-Lex metadata gives its date as 8 July 2026, Official Journal publication 24 July 2026, entry into force 27 July 2026.
Source: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, European Parliament and Council, July 2026. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng

## Claim-by-claim findings

**1. Article 4 duty.** Corrected. The original wording (ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy) has been replaced. Providers and deployers must now "take measures to support the development of AI literacy" of staff and others using AI systems on their behalf, and the text states they need not guarantee any specific level for any individual. The Commission and Member States must support providers and deployers, and the AI Board adopts recommendations.
Source: Article 4, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-4
Confirmed by the amending text: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, European Parliament and Council, July 2026. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202601744

**2. "Assessment records are evidence of Article 4 compliance."** Softened. The Commission says no certificate is needed and that internal records of trainings and other initiatives are sufficient. An assessment is one input to those records, not the whole of them. The guide now says this.
Source: AI literacy questions and answers (Article 4), European Commission, May 2025 (page last updated October 2026). https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers

**3. Article 5 examples.** Corrected. Point (c) prohibits social scoring as defined. Point (f) prohibits inferring emotions of a person in the workplace and education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons, so "emotion recognition at work" was too broad. The Omnibus added two new prohibitions (points (ba) and (bb): non-consensual intimate depictions and child sexual abuse material), applying from 2 December 2026.
Source: Article 5, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5
Dates: Article 113, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-113

**4. Fundamental rights impact assessment "for high-risk deployers".** Corrected. Article 27(1) applies to bodies governed by public law, private entities providing public services, and deployers of certain Annex III systems (points 5(b) and 5(c)), not to all high-risk deployers. The Omnibus replaced paragraphs 4 and 5 (cross-reference to data protection impact assessments; AI Office questionnaire template).
Source: Article 27, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-27

**5. Article 9 risk management system.** Reworded. Article 9(1) is written passively ("a risk management system shall be established ... in relation to high-risk AI systems"). Article 16(a) puts the duty to ensure compliance with Articles 8 to 15 on providers, so the indicator now refers to systems the organisation provides.
Source: Article 9, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-9
Source: Article 16, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-16

**6. Article 10 data governance.** Reworded to the text. Article 10(3) requires data sets to be relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible free of errors and complete. Bias is dealt with in Article 10(2)(f) as an examination for possible biases. The Omnibus replaced paragraphs 1 and 6 and deleted paragraph 5; paragraphs 2 and 3 were not marked as changed.
Source: Article 10, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-10

**7. Log retention "minimum 6 months".** Confirmed, with scope added. Providers (Article 19) and deployers (Article 26(6)) of high-risk systems keep logs automatically generated by the system, to the extent the logs are under their control, for a period appropriate to the intended purpose and at least six months, unless other Union or national law says otherwise (in particular data protection law). Neither article is marked as amended.
Source: Article 19, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-19
Source: Article 26, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26

**8. Articles 11 and 13.** Reworded. Article 11(1) requires technical documentation to be drawn up before a high-risk system is placed on the market or put into service and kept up to date (marked amended; the change concerns simplified forms for smaller businesses). Article 13 requires systems to be transparent enough for deployers to interpret output, and to come with instructions for use. A deployer therefore holds the provider's instructions for use; the technical documentation is a provider task.
Source: Article 11, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-11
Source: Article 13, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-13

**9. Articles 10, 12 and 17 "organisation-wide".** Reworded. Article 12(1) requires high-risk systems to technically allow automatic recording of events. Article 17(1) requires providers to put a quality management system in place; the Omnibus replaced Article 17(2) so that implementation is proportionate to the size of the provider's organisation. Indicator now limited to systems the organisation provides.
Source: Article 12, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-12
Source: Article 17, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-17

**10. "Meaningful human oversight that can override outputs".** Reworded. Article 14(4)(d) is a design requirement (the person overseeing can disregard, override or reverse output). The deployer duty is Article 26(2): assign human oversight to people with the necessary competence, training and authority. Indicators now cite Article 26(2).
Source: Article 14, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-14
Source: Article 26, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26

**11. "Ensures all deployed tools meet conformity requirements".** Corrected. Conformity assessment under Article 43 is a provider duty (Article 16(f)). Deployers must use systems in line with the instructions for use (Article 26(1)) and monitor operation (Article 26(5)). Indicator now says this and asks vendors for evidence of conformity.
Source: Article 43, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-43
Source: Article 16, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-16

**12. "per August 2026 requirements".** Corrected. The Act applies generally from 2 August 2026, but the Omnibus moved the high-risk obligations: 2 December 2027 for Annex III uses (Article 6(2)) and 2 August 2028 for Annex I products (Article 6(1)). Prohibited practices and AI literacy have applied since 2 February 2025.
Source: Article 113, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-113
Source: AI Act (policy page), European Commission, October 2026 (page last updated 19 October 2026). https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai

**13. "2026 to 2027 deadlines".** Replaced. The indicator now says the Act has already been amended once in 2026, rather than naming dates that can go stale.

**14. Provider versus deployer roles.** Addressed across the indicators above. Several Practitioner and Champion indicators now say whether they apply to systems the organisation provides or to high-risk systems it deploys.

## Other changes made

- **Risk levels renamed.** The Commission describes four levels: unacceptable, high, transparency and minimal or no risk. The June text said "limited". Source: AI Act (policy page), European Commission, October 2026. https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
- **EU tag removed from two indicators** where I could not tie the wording to an AI Act article: not entering personal or confidential data into unauthorised tools (a data protection and policy point), and building a culture with escalation paths.
- **Post-market monitoring.** Article 72(1) puts this on providers of high-risk systems, so that indicator now says so. Source: Article 72, AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at July 2026), European Commission. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-72

## Limits of this check

- I read article wording through the Commission's AI Act Service Desk, which says it reproduces the EUR-Lex consolidated text as at 27 July 2026. I did not open the EUR-Lex consolidated document itself. The authentic text is on EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng
- The Omnibus text on EUR-Lex was read for about the first 100,000 of roughly 149,656 characters. Amendments to Articles 12, 14 and 26 were not visible in that part; the Service Desk pages for those articles showed no amendment markers.
- The operative wording of Article 113 was read on the Service Desk and the Commission policy page, which agree with each other and with the Omnibus recital on the 2027 and 2028 dates. I did not read Article 113 on EUR-Lex.
- Sources disagree slightly on the Omnibus entry into force: the Commission's literacy Q&A says mid-July 2026, EUR-Lex metadata and the Commission policy page say 27 July 2026. The report uses 27 July.
- Indicators that mention regulators in general terms (for example engaging with standards bodies) were not tested against a specific article.
- The Act has already changed once this year. Re-check before each new client engagement, then update `meta.verifiedDate` and `meta.verifiedAgainst` in `data/framework.json`.
